Finance Optimizer · market data infrastructure

The pipeline's job is to stop.

This dataset decides which card someone swipes and where they park their savings. A wrong cashback rate doesn't throw an error — it produces a fluent, confident, wrong recommendation. So every stage below is built to hold data back: block on a conflict, force a confidence score down, refuse to rank a card it can't value. What follows is what each gate catches and why it exists.

Where the data lives

The JSON files in git are the source of truth. The Google Sheet is a generated view of them. This matters because the Sheet used to be editable and live — which meant a reviewed dataset and a hand-edited one could quietly diverge with no record of which was right.

canonical
data/market/*.json

Seven tables in git. One commit per approved run, so every change has a diff, an author and a rollback.

generated
Sheet market tabs

Written by publish.py. Safe to read, safe to overwrite. Hand-edits are allowed for urgent fixes but get caught by the drift check.

sheet-native
Users · UserProducts · Movements

The app writes these. The pipeline never touches them.

Why hand-edits are still allowed. A strict one-way pipeline has no path for an urgent correction. So the Sheet stays editable, and Stage 0 compares it against canonical before anything is published. Drift is reported cell by cell and must be back-ported or discarded — publishing over it is how a correct manual fix gets lost.

03

How confident is a number?

Confidence is computed, never judged. A person deciding "this feels solid" produces a different answer next week; a rule produces the same one. Every field group carries a score derived from how many independent sources back it, what kind they are, and how old they are.

high

Cost, rewards and yield need two independent sources. They feed the recommendation directly, so an error there produces wrong advice rather than a wrong display. Single-sourcing doesn't block publishing — it caps the score below high, which is the real protection. Blocking would leave the app with no data instead of data marked uncertain.
04

Seven things that look simpler than they are

Each of these came from a real row that broke. They're the parts most likely to be re-broken by someone who hasn't hit them yet.

A card is active, closed, or gone

Three states, not a boolean. Cards closed to new applicants are still held by existing customers — the advisor needs them, New Picks must exclude them. A binary flag breaks one of those two features whichever way you set it.

A boost is a replacement, usually

Mexican accounts quote totals. "Up to 13%" means 13% all-in, not 13 points on top of 7. The old engine added them.

Ualá paid 21.75% instead of 15% — $1,688/yr overstated on a $25,000 balance.

A conditional rate is not a tier

Ualá publishes 6.75%, 12% and 15% — all covering the same $0–30,000 band. They're selected by behaviour, not balance. Recorded as tiers they overlap, break contiguity, and leave the engine no way to know which rate a customer earns.

effective_rate_pct is the only comparable rate

BBVA Azul earns 9% — in points BBVA publishes no peso value for. Ranked against a 2% cashback card on the raw number, it wins on a value nobody can spend.

10 of 26 mapped cards return "not comparable" rather than a guess.

Insurance follows the issuer's licence

Never researched per product. banco → IPAB 400k UDIS, sofipo → PROSOFIPO 25k, ifpe → none. Stored in UDIS, never pesos — a regulator page still quotes a peso figure roughly 35% below today's value.

A licence can be granted before it takes effect

Nu was authorised as a bank on 10 July and began operating on 6 August. In between, the registry showed "banca múltiple" while customer money was still under PROSOFIPO. pending_conversion holds both, and errors once the date passes without promotion.

UNKNOWN is always acceptable

A guessed number never is. Sentinels — UNKNOWN, NOT_APPLICABLE, UNCAPPED — share columns with numbers, so anything doing arithmetic must branch on type first. Coercing a sentinel to a default is how an unknown becomes a fact.

Where the numbers come from

Regulators give you the skeleton. Issuers give you the flesh. The two are used differently: enumerate products from the issuer, then reconcile against the regulator to find what the issuer didn't advertise. Never concatenate the lists — produce a diff.

The regulator is the completeness check, not the detail source. An issuer's site won't show cards closed to new applicants, invitation-only tiers, or quietly retired products — but the institution filed those with CONDUSEF itself. That's why the reconciliation runs in that direction. Note also that CNBV and CONDUSEF portals block automated access, so Stage 1 needs a person to pull the registry.

What the engine got wrong

Correct data still produces wrong advice if the engine reads it wrong. Seven fixes, each measured against the migrated dataset.

BugWhat the user saw
Unsourced point value defaulted to 1 peso/pointA 9%-in-points card outranked real 2% cashback
Yield boosts added to the base rateUalá shown at 21.75% instead of 15%
Monthly cap compared against one transaction$500/mo cap paid out $1,200 in a month
Category bonus always replaced the base ratereplaces_or_adds_to_base never read
One boost per accountUalá's three non-stacking routes unrepresentable

An unmet condition is advice, not a missing value. Rather than silently scoring Ualá at 6.75%, the engine now returns what unlocks the better rate and what it's worth — +$2,063/yr on a $25,000 balance. That turns the gap in what we know about a customer into the most useful screen in the product.
